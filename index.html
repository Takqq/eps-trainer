NAIT DMIT Quick Reference: High-Yield Codec & Editing Cheat Sheet

Master terminology, compression principles, and audio engineering specs at a glance.

📦 Codec vs Container (Wrapper)

Container (.mp4, .mov, .mkv, .avi, .dav): The file wrapper that holds video streams, audio tracks, and metadata together.

Codec (H.264, H.265/HEVC, ProRes): The algorithm that compresses and decompresses raw pixel data. An MP4 file will not play if the computer lacks the underlying video codec.

Remuxing vs Transcoding: Remuxing re-wraps the streams into a new container without re-encoding (instant, zero quality loss). Transcoding decompresses and re-encodes (slow, lossy).

📺 Interlaced (1080i) vs Progressive (1080p)

Interlaced (1080i): Records alternating odd and even horizontal scan lines at separate fractions of a second. Fast motion creates jagged horizontal "combing" or "mice teeth" artifacts.

Progressive (1080p): Draws all horizontal lines sequentially as a full solid frame. Always deinterlace legacy surveillance video before courtroom presentation.

🧩 GOP Structure (I, P, and B-Frames)

I-Frame (Intra / Keyframe): Full, self-contained complete image. Doesn't depend on other frames.

P-Frame (Predicted): Stores only pixel changes from previous frames.

B-Frame (Bi-directional): Stores differences based on past and future frames. Rule: Cutting without re-encoding must snap to an I-frame, or you get grey macroblocking smearing.

⏱️ Timecode: Drop-Frame (DF) vs Non-Drop-Frame (NDF)

29.97 fps Reality: NTSC video runs slightly slower than 30 fps. NDF timecode counts 30 fps rigidly, gaining 3.6 seconds of drift per hour (~30 seconds over an 8-hour shift).

Drop-Frame Solution: Drops two frame numbers (not actual video) every minute (except every 10th min) so the display matches real-world clocks.

🔊 Digital Audio Engineering Standards

Standard: 48 kHz at 16-bit or 24-bit is the universal video/police evidence standard (44.1 kHz is consumer CD audio).

0 dBFS Clipping: 0 dBFS is the absolute digital ceiling. Exceeding it flattens waveform peaks into harsh, permanent distortion.

Filters: High-Pass (sub-80 Hz cut) eliminates engine/wind rumble. 60 Hz Notch Filter cuts AC electrical hum. Dynamic Compression squashes peaks so faint whispers become audible.

🎯 Adobe Premiere Pro Masking & Keyframes

Linear vs Bezier: Change Spatial Interpolation from Auto-Bezier to Linear to prevent masks from arcing in weird loops during face tracking.

Mask Expansion & Feather: Expansion enlarges the blur perimeter over hair/ears; Feathering softens the edge from harsh cookie-cutter lines to smooth gradients.

Video Levels: Rec. 709 Legal (16–235) vs Full (0–255). Full range sent to legal monitors crushes dark shadows to pure black.

Video Codecs Spectrum (Ranked 1 to 8: Most Efficient to Highest Quality)

RANK 1 • MOST EFFICIENT AV1 (AOMedia Video 1)
Extreme Compression • Next-Gen Open Standard

Technical Profile: Open-source, royalty-free inter-frame codec developed by the Alliance for Open Media (Google, Netflix, Apple, Intel, Microsoft). Delivers approx. 30% greater efficiency than HEVC/H.265 and 50% greater than H.264 for identical visual resolution.

DEMU & Police Context: Extremely slow to encode, but produces the smallest possible file sizes. Increasingly deployed for cloud evidence archiving and high-volume surveillance streaming to minimize municipal cloud storage fees.

RANK 2 • ULTRA-HIGH EFFICIENCY HEVC / H.265 (High Efficiency Video Coding)
4K / 8K Surveillance Standard

Technical Profile: Modern inter-frame successor to H.264. Utilizes Coding Tree Units (CTUs) up to 64×64 pixels (versus rigid 16×16 macroblocks in H.264), cutting file sizes in half (~50% bitrate reduction) while preserving high detail in 4K resolutions.

DEMU & Police Context: Standard for modern 4K commercial CCTV systems, drone footage, and newer Axon Body 4 hardware configurations. Requires newer PC hardware decoders; older court laptops may struggle with choppy playback.

RANK 3 • THE UNIVERSAL STANDARD AVC / H.264 (MPEG-4 Part 10)
100% Global Compatibility • Crown Disclosure Standard

Technical Profile: The undisputed champion of digital video worldwide. Inter-frame compression using 16×16 macroblocks, variable frame rates, and wide profile support (Baseline, Main, High). Balances excellent image clarity with fast encoding and minimal CPU playback overhead.

DEMU & Police Context: The primary format for Axon Body 3/4 cameras, Evidence.com, and Alberta Crown prosecution disclosure. Every computer in the justice system can decode H.264 instantly without special codecs.

RANK 4 • LEGACY DIGITAL MPEG-4 Part 2 (DivX / Xvid)
Early 2000s Standard

Technical Profile: Older DCT-based inter-frame compression. Substantially less efficient than H.264; requires higher bitrates to avoid blocking artifacts.

DEMU & Police Context: Frequently encountered when transcoding legacy retail surveillance exports or historical police case files recorded in the mid-2000s.

RANK 5 • BROADCAST / DVD MPEG-2 (H.262)
Physical DVD & Over-the-Air TV Standard

Technical Profile: The 1995 standard designed for commercial DVD-Video discs and broadcast television. Requires heavy bitrates (5–9 Mbps for standard definition 480i/720x480) with noticeable macroblocking during rapid movement.

DEMU & Police Context: Used when burning physical DVD-R video discs for defense counsel who lack secure digital evidence portal access or when dealing with legacy police interrogation room DVD recorders.

RANK 6 • INTRA-FRAME PHOTO STREAM Motion JPEG (MJPEG)
Legacy Security CCTV • Discrete JPEG Frames

Technical Profile: Intra-frame only. Every frame of video is compressed as an individual, self-contained JPEG photograph with zero temporal compression (no P/B frames). Extremely large file size for low resolutions.

DEMU & Police Context: Common in older commercial security DVRs, bank ATM cameras, and traffic intersection cameras. Great for stopping on a single frame to extract a clean still image, but storage-intensive.

RANK 7 • EDITING INTERMEDIATE (VISUALLY LOSSLESS) Apple ProRes (422 / HQ) & Avid DNxHR / DNxHD
All-I-Frame • Multi-Pass Post-Production Master

Technical Profile: Intra-frame (every frame is an independent I-frame) with 10-bit color depth and 4:2:2 chroma subsampling. Bitrates range from 100 to 220+ Mbps at 1080p (~1 GB per minute). Virtually zero generational loss across repeated renders.

DEMU & Police Context: The master working format inside Adobe Premiere Pro. When performing complex facial tracking masks, forensic color adjustments, or multi-angle synchronization, converting to ProRes/DNx eliminates timeline scrub lag and prevents compression degradation before the final H.264 court export.

RANK 8 • MATHEMATICALLY LOSSLESS / RAW FFV1 / Uncompressed YUV (4:2:2 / 4:4:4)
100% Bit-Accurate • Forensic Archive Standard

Technical Profile: Zero loss of pixel data. Every single byte captured by the sensor is mathematically preserved without perceptual loss or discard. Generates gigantic files (tens of gigabytes per minute).

DEMU & Police Context: FFV1 is the internationally recognized open standard for permanent digital preservation in legal and government archives. Used in forensic laboratories when analyzing microscopic details (e.g., license plate enhancement or facial reconstruction) where lossy compression cannot be tolerated.

Audio Codecs Spectrum (Ranked 1 to 6: Most Efficient to Highest Quality)

RANK 1 • MOST EFFICIENT Opus
Modern Open Standard • Superior Speech at Low Bitrates

Technical Profile: Highly versatile lossy audio codec developed by Xiph.Org and IETF. Adapts dynamically from ultra-low bitrate narrowband speech (6 kbps) up to high-fidelity stereo audio (128 kbps). Superior speech clarity over MP3 and AAC at low bitrates.

DEMU & Police Context: Used in modern VoIP dispatch recording, police radio IP streaming, and WebRTC body camera live-streaming back to dispatch commanders.

RANK 2 • UNIVERSAL VIDEO AUDIO AAC (Advanced Audio Coding - LC / HE-AAC)
MP4 Default Standard • High Speech & Music Fidelity

Technical Profile: Designed to succeed MP3, delivering significantly higher audio fidelity at lower bitrates (typically 96–192 kbps). Uses modified discrete cosine transform (MDCT) with superior transient response.

DEMU & Police Context: The universal audio codec inside all Axon Body camera MP4 files and court disclosure packages. Cleanly balances vocal clarity with compact storage across thousands of shift hours.

RANK 3 • LEGACY CONSUMER STANDARD MP3 (MPEG-1 Audio Layer III)
1993 Consumer Format • Universal Playback

Technical Profile: The historic standard for digital music. Less efficient than AAC; low bitrates (128 kbps or lower) introduce harsh "swishy" compression artifacts and aggressively cut high frequencies above 16 kHz.

DEMU & Police Context: Frequently found on civilian phone recordings, undercover digital voice recorders, and 911 audio extractions submitted by external telecom providers.

RANK 4 • MULTI-CHANNEL SURROUND AC-3 (Dolby Digital)
DVD / Broadcast Multi-Channel Standard

Technical Profile: Broadcast lossy audio codec supporting up to 5.1 discrete audio channels (left, center, right, left surround, right surround, LFE subwoofer) at bitrates between 384 and 640 kbps.

DEMU & Police Context: Encountered when authoring physical court DVD-Video exhibits or ingesting multi-channel surveillance recordings from high-end facility security arrays.

RANK 5 • COMPRESSED LOSSLESS FLAC (Free Lossless Audio Codec) & ALAC (Apple Lossless)
Bit-Accurate Compression • 40–50% Size Reduction

Technical Profile: Functions exactly like a ZIP file for audio data. Reduces file size by 40–50% compared to raw WAV, but when decoded, 100% of the original audio waveform is bit-for-bit identical. Zero loss of dynamic range, frequency response, or acoustic transients.

DEMU & Police Context: Ideal for long-term storage of sensitive wiretaps, recorded 911 calls, and covert room probes where lossless evidentiary integrity is mandatory without consuming uncompressed storage space.

RANK 6 • UNCOMPRESSED STUDIO MASTER Linear PCM / WAV (48 kHz / 16-bit or 24-bit)
Forensic Audio Master • Zero Compression • Maximum Quality

Technical Profile: Pure, raw uncompressed digital audio representation (Pulse Code Modulation). Standard video format: 48,000 samples per second at 16-bit or 24-bit depth (~1.5 Mbps for stereo). Zero compression algorithm applied; stores acoustic waveforms directly as binary voltage measurements.

DEMU & Police Context: The mandatory working format inside WavePad and Adobe Premiere Pro when performing acoustic speech filtering, 60 Hz hum removal, or forensic volume leveling. Technicians always export and process in uncompressed WAV before encoding the final disclosed MP4.

Adobe Creative Cloud vs. AVS Converter & WavePad Sound Editor

How your professional Adobe foundation (Premiere, Audition, Media Encoder) translates directly to the police evidence workflow.

100% Skill Translatability
💡 Why Police Units Use AVS Converter & WavePad

Adobe Creative Cloud is broadcast-grade and powerful, but it can be rigid with corrupted or non-standard merchant CCTV files. If a convenience store robbery exports Dahua .dav, Samsung .sec, or raw elementary streams lacking index headers, Adobe Media Encoder will often reject them. AVS Video Converter and WavePad Sound Editor are fast, forgiving, single-purpose utilities used in police units to batch-transcode exotic formats and perform quick 60-second audio bleeping without the overhead of heavy project files.

🔄

AVS Video Converter

The CCTV Batch Transcoder

Adobe Media Encoder Equivalent
What It Does in Law Enforcement: Ingests proprietary security camera footage (.dav, .sec, .asf, .avi, raw .h264) and batch-converts them into standardized, court-ready MP4/H.264 containers that play reliably on Crown and defense computers.
Core UI & Workflow Steps:
  • Format Bar: Top buttons (To MP4, To AVI, To MOV). Police standard is almost always To MP4.
  • Profile Presets: Dropdown with pre-configured settings (e.g. Full HD 1080p: H.264/AVC, 29.97 fps, 4000 kbps, Audio AAC).
  • Advanced / Edit Options: Modify frame rate, aspect ratio (fixing squished 4:3 security video to 16:9), or audio bitrate.
  • Batch Processing: Drop 20 CCTV files in, set destination folder, hit Convert Now!
⚠️ Critical Evidence Rule: In AVS Converter, always ensure Frame Rate is set to "Original". Never accidentally convert 15 fps surveillance to 30 fps, or temporal frame blending will compromise forensic clarity!
🔊

WavePad Sound Editor

The Audio Redaction & Bleep Tool

Adobe Audition (Waveform) Equivalent
What It Does in Law Enforcement: Destructive single-track waveform editor for rapid audio redaction of 911 calls, wiretaps, dispatch tapes, and extracted BWV audio. Used to mute names, insert standard court bleeps, and mask confidential informant voices.
Core UI & Redaction Commands:
  • Scrub & Highlight: Visual waveform view (just like Audition's Waveform Editor Shift+6). Click and drag to select sensitive audio.
  • Silence / Mute: Highlight selection → press Ctrl+L or Edit > Silence Selection. Flattens audio to 0 dB.
  • Insert 1 kHz Tone / Bleep: Edit > Insert > Tone / Frequency Generator. Generates standard 1,000 Hz sine wave tone over the selection.
  • Pitch Shift (Voice Masking): Effects > Pitch > Shift Pitch. Raises or lowers vocal timbre by 3-5 semitones without altering tempo (shields informant identities under R. v. Leipert).
  • Noise Gate / High-Pass: Filters out engine rumble, AC hum, and radio hiss for courtroom audibility.
⚖️ Courtroom Audio Standard: Always export working audio redactions as uncompressed Linear PCM / WAV (48 kHz / 16-bit or 24-bit) to maintain acoustic integrity.

Freedom of Information and Protection of Privacy Act (FOIP - Alberta)

Provincial legislation governing all public bodies, including Edmonton Police Service and City of Edmonton.

Section 17(1) — Personal Privacy Privacy Balancing Test

What it protects: Prevents the "unreasonable invasion of a third party's personal privacy."

  • Uninvolved Bystanders: Pedestrians walking by a traffic stop, customers sitting in an LRT train car, neighbors watching from apartment doorways.
  • Medical & Health Records: Prescription labels, open hospital triage charts, Alberta Personal Health Numbers (PHN).
  • Private Financial Data: Credit card numbers, banking receipts, Social Insurance Numbers.
  • Unlisted Identifiers: Personal cell phone numbers, private home addresses written on letters on a coffee table.
Technician Action: Apply Gaussian blur or Mosaic pixelation to faces/screens; insert 1,000 Hz tone over spoken phone numbers or home addresses.
Section 18 — Individual or Public Safety Life & Physical Safety

What it protects: Withholds information if disclosure could reasonably be expected to threaten the safety or physical/mental health of an individual.

  • Safe Houses & Domestic Shelters: Street addresses, building entrances, or interior layouts of women's emergency shelters or temporary witness safe houses.
  • Persons in Acute Crisis: Individuals in psychiatric distress, suicide attempts, or severe mental health episodes where disclosure would cause severe psychological harm.
  • Targeted Victims: Stalking victims' new employment locations or unlisted vehicle licence plates.
Technician Action: Solid black mask over building address signs, pamphlets with crisis shelter phone lines, and room interiors.
Section 20 — Law Enforcement Harm Test Tactics & Officer Safety

What it protects: Withholds records that could harm a law enforcement matter or reveal confidential tactical techniques.

  • Cruiser Mobile Data Terminals (MDT): In-car computer screens displaying active CAD queues, internal police cell lines, CPIC queries, or officer notes.
  • Specialized Tactical Methods: EPS Tactical Unit (SWAT) breaching mechanisms, covert surveillance camera placements, specialized entry hand signals.
  • Police Communications: Unpublished direct divisional telephone extensions, tactical radio talk-groups, access door security PIN codes.
Technician Action: Solid opaque mask over the entire MDT computer display whenever the officer glances at the center console.
Section 27 — Legal Advice & Crown Privilege Solicitor-Client Privilege

What it protects: Protects privileged communications between public body employees and legal counsel.

  • Crown Consultation: Body camera footage where an arresting officer telephones a Crown prosecutor to discuss charge viability, search warrant wording, or legal trial strategy.
  • Internal Police Legal: Legal advice rendered by City of Edmonton or EPS legal advisors to sworn members.
Technician Action: Mute or redact the verbal audio stream of the telephone consultation and log as "FOIP s.27 Legal Privilege".

Youth Criminal Justice Act (YCJA) & PIPA Boundaries

Federal protections for young persons and jurisdictional boundaries between public vs private data.

YCJA Section 110 — Youth Identity Protection Federal Criminal Statute

Core Principle: Strict statutory prohibition against publishing or disclosing the name or identifying features of a young person (under 18) dealt with under the YCJA.

  • All Roles Protected: Applies equally to youth accused/suspects, youth victims, and youth witnesses.
  • What to Redact: Facial features, distinctive hair/tattoos, full names spoken aloud, school uniforms/logos, student IDs, parent names, home addresses.
  • Longevity (Crucial!): Protection does not expire when the youth turns 18. It remains permanently protected unless an adult sentence was formally ordered by a youth justice court.
Technician Rule: If in doubt whether a person is 17 or 18, check CAD/RMS DOB. If under 18, redact 100% of identifying features.
PIPA vs FOIP — Jurisdictional Boundary Provincial Jurisdiction

Core Distinction: Knowing which privacy law applies to private enterprise vs municipal police.

  • PIPA (Personal Information Protection Act): Applies to private sector organizations in Alberta (businesses, bars, convenience stores, landlords, private malls). Governs how a store records CCTV.
  • FOIP: Applies to public bodies (EPS, City of Edmonton, provincial departments).
  • The Evidence Handover: When a business provides private CCTV to EPS, custody transitions to police. FOIP, the Criminal Code, and Stinchcombe govern how DEMU redacts and discloses that video to court.
Test Takeaway: DEMU operates under FOIP; private businesses operate under PIPA.

Supreme Court Landmark Precedents & Charter Rights

Judicial rulings that define Crown disclosure, informant protection, and courtroom speed.

R. v. Stinchcombe (1991)
First-Party Disclosure Duty

The Rule: The Crown has an absolute constitutional duty to disclose all relevant first-party material to the defense—both inculpatory (supporting guilt) and exculpatory (supporting innocence).

Police cannot withhold, delete, or selectively edit body camera footage because an officer made an error or the video contradicts an officer's notes. The court decides what is relevant; the technician must preserve the entire unedited master.

R. v. Leipert (1997)
Informer Privilege

The Rule: Informer Privilege is an absolute legal shield. It belongs to the Crown and the informant, and cannot be waived by a police officer. There is almost zero judicial exception (only "innocence at stake").

If body camera footage captures a confidential informant talking to police, the technician must apply solid visual masks, audio frequency pitch-shifting, and redact location clues to prevent any possibility of identification.

R. v. Jordan (2016) & CC S.503
Court Timeline Constraints

R. v. Jordan: Sets strict ceilings for trials: 18 months for provincial courts; 30 months for superior courts. Disclosure delays by police cause charges to be stayed (dismissed).

Criminal Code S.503 (Bail): An accused in custody must be brought before a justice within 24 hours. Video disclosure for bail hearings is priority #1 in the technician queue.

Evidence Acts & Generally Accepted Recordkeeping Principles (GARP)

Rules governing electronic document admissibility, system integrity certificates, and digital preservation.

Canada Evidence Act (s. 31.1) & Alberta Evidence Act
Presumption of Electronic Document Integrity

Section 31.1 Canada Evidence Act: The integrity of an electronic document is legally presumed if it is established that the electronic records storage system was operating properly at all material times.

Certificate of Document Integrity: The DEMU technician issues a formal statutory certificate verifying that Evidence.com maintained write-once cloud storage, recorded automated audit trails, and SHA-256 cryptographic hashes matched ingest.

Form 5.2 (Report to a Justice): When police seize physical digital media (DVDs, USBs, SD cards), Criminal Code Section 489.1 requires filing a Form 5.2 to account to the court for the physical item.

Generally Accepted Recordkeeping Principles (GARP)
Information Governance Standard

Key Principles Applied Daily in DEMU:

  • Principle of Integrity: Reasonable guarantee of authenticity through cryptographic checksums (SHA-256) and immutable write-once master storage.
  • Principle of Protection: Restricting access to sensitive files (Restricted Access Groups) to protect confidentiality.
  • Principle of Retention: Maintaining files according to statutory limitation periods (Summary: 2–3 yrs; Indictable: 10 yrs; Homicide: Indefinite).
  • Principle of Disposition: Deleting non-evidentiary files only through authorized, audited administrative workflows (never rogue field deletions).
  • Principle of Accountability: Automatic logging of every single user click, view, edit, or download.
