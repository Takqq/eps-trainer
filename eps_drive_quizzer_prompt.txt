DOMAIN 1: ADVANCED STATUTORY REDACTIONS & EVIDENCE LAW
Q1: You are disclosing an impaired driving arrest video. During cruiser transport, the suspect calls their lawyer from the back seat, and their conversation is captured by the camera. How do you handle this audio?
[Answer: Redact and mute the entire conversation under Solicitor-Client Privilege (FOIP s. 27 / common law); this privilege is absolute and can never be disclosed to Crown or defense.]

Q2: An officer's body camera captures paramedics treating an overdose victim in an ambulance at a crime scene. The patient is not a suspect. What redactions are required?
[Answer: Blur the patient's face, obscure visible injuries/medical treatments, and mute personal health details under FOIP s. 17 personal privacy and the Health Information Act (HIA).]

Q3: An undercover officer appears for 5 seconds in an arrest video handing handcuffs to a patrol member. What statutory law governs their redaction?
[Answer: FOIP Section 20(1)(g) / Section 18: Protecting law enforcement techniques and preventing endangerment to the life or safety of undercover personnel.]

Q4: Crown counsel requests an unredacted copy of an assault video, while defense counsel requests their disclosure package. Do both parties receive the same file?
[Answer: No. Crown receives the unredacted master with proposed redaction markers. Defense receives only the Crown-approved redacted derivative copy with a disclosure certificate.]

Q5: An anonymous Crime Stoppers audio tip is ingested regarding an armed robbery. Defense demands disclosure of the audio to assess witness credibility. What Supreme Court case prohibits this?
[Answer: R. v. Leipert (1997) establishes that informer privilege is absolute; the audio must be withheld or completely redacted to protect the tipster's identity.]

Q6: A 17-year-old youth is arrested alongside an adult for robbery. The adult's trial is proceeding in adult criminal court. How does YCJA Section 110 apply to the video entered into evidence?
[Answer: YCJA s. 110 publication ban still fully applies to the youth; you must blur the youth's face, distinctive marks/clothing, and pitch-shift their voice, even in the adult co-accused's trial.]

Q7: What is the core difference between first-party disclosure under R. v. Stinchcombe and third-party disclosure under R. v. O'Connor?
[Answer: Stinchcombe mandates automatic disclosure of evidence in possession of police/Crown unless clearly irrelevant; O'Connor governs records held by third parties (medical/therapy), requiring a formal court application and judicial review.]

Q8: An officer faces disciplinary charges under the Police Act. In an unrelated criminal case, defense requests the officer's internal disciplinary history. What landmark case governs this?
[Answer: R. v. McNeil (2009) mandates disclosure of findings of serious misconduct against involved police officers that could impact their credibility.]

DOMAIN 2: FORENSIC VIDEO/AUDIO CODECS & INGESTION ENGINEERING
Q9: A witness smartphone video (.MOV) imported into Premiere Pro drifts out of sync by 3 seconds over 5 minutes. What technical property caused this?
[Answer: Variable Frame Rate (VFR); transcoding the video to a Constant Frame Rate (CFR, e.g., 29.97 or 30 fps) locks audio and video in sync.]

Q10: Surveillance video exported at 720x480 resolution makes suspects look unnaturally wide and short on a computer screen. What setting is incorrect?
[Answer: Pixel Aspect Ratio (PAR); standard NTSC uses non-square rectangular pixels (0.91 or 1.21), which distort if rendered as 1.0 square pixels on PC monitors.]

Q11: A convenience store owner provides surveillance in a proprietary .dav format that won't play in standard Windows players. What are your first two forensic steps?
[Answer: 1. Calculate and log the SHA-256 hash of the raw original .dav file. 2. Export a transcode or container conversion using forensic software while preserving the untouched master.]

Q12: A cruiser dashcam recording of a passing vehicle shows jagged horizontal comb lines around the car edges. What is this artifact and how is it corrected?
[Answer: Interlacing combing artifacts (1080i/480i); corrected by applying a deinterlacing filter (Yadif or interpolate) to convert odd/even fields into progressive frames.]

Q13: What is the technical difference between remuxing and transcoding a video file?
[Answer: Remuxing changes the container format (e.g. MKV to MP4) without re-encoding video streams (lossless and instant); transcoding decompresses and re-compresses the bitstream, causing generational loss.]

Q14: When redacting a police radio code or name in WavePad, why is inserting a 1 kHz tone bleep or pitch-shifting preferred over absolute digital silence?
[Answer: Complete digital silence can appear as foul play or dropped signal in court; tone bleeps or pitch-shifts preserve ambient room acoustics while obscuring words.]

Q15: What is the visual consequence of playing video recorded in Broadcast Legal Range (16-235) on a monitor expecting Full Range (0-255) without color remapping?
[Answer: Crushed shadows and clipped highlights; dark alley scenes look excessively murky and black details are lost.]

Q16: In video compression, what is an I-Frame compared to a P-Frame?
[Answer: An I-frame (Intra) is a full standalone image containing complete picture data; a P-frame (Predicted) only stores differences and motion vectors from previous frames.]

Q18: Evidence from an internal Professional Standards Branch (PSB) investigation is ingested into Evidence.com. How do you prevent unauthorized internal viewing?

Q19: How should DEMU share a multi-gigabyte body-worn video package with an external partner agency like the RCMP?
[Answer: Use Evidence.com Partner Agency Sharing to share the digital package securely within the cloud platform, avoiding unencrypted USB drives or external re-compression.]

Q20: A body-worn video file has two tags: 'Traffic Violation' (2-year retention) and 'Use of Force' (Indefinite retention). When will the system delete the file?

DOMAIN 4: CAD VS. RMS DISCREPANCY SPOTTING & DATA INTEGRITY
Q25: CAD lists a call at 14:30 MDT, but the body camera internal timestamp displays 20:30. Is the camera clock broken?
[Answer: No; the camera displays Coordinated Universal Time (UTC); Mountain Daylight Time (MDT) is UTC minus 6 hours (14:30 MDT = 20:30 UTC).]

Q26: CAD shows Unit 12 (Badge 2410) was dispatched as primary, but DEMS shows Badge 3180 recorded the initial arrival. How do you verify this?
[Answer: Review CAD unit dispatch history for secondary cover units; Badge 3180 was likely a secondary unit that arrived first on scene, requiring both badges to be indexed.]

Q27: A CAD sheet lists occurrence number 26-084920, but the video ingest metadata shows 25-084920. What is this error?
[Answer: A year rollover discrepancy (2025 vs 2026); correct the DEMS index to match the true 2026 occurrence year and log the correction note.]

Q28: Two 911 calls for the same disturbance are merged into CAD Event #101. How should DEMU tag the resulting video?
[Answer: Cross-reference and index both CAD event numbers in the metadata search tags so investigators can locate the evidence using either identifier.]

Q29: An incident is classified in CAD as an Indictable Assault, but the officer assigned a 1-year 'Unfounded / Minor' retention tag. What must the clerk do?
[Answer: Upgrade the retention tag in DEMS to Indictable / Serious Offense (10-Year or Indefinite) and notify the officer or unit supervisor.]

Q30: An officer stops a vehicle two blocks away from the original CAD dispatch location. How is this recorded in the disclosure index?
